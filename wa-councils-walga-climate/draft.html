WA Councils' Climate Policy-Making After the WALGA Declaration

Project 13 of the adhi-climate portfolio. This page previews where the project stands before it earns a public story card: the literature review is done, the document analysis is not, and the numbers below are read from a draft, not a finished result.

What this project asks

After a Western Australian local government signs WALGA's Western Australian Local Government Declaration on Climate Change, what changes, if any, occur in that council's climate policy-making, and do those changes resemble the outcomes reported in the climate-emergency-declaration literature, or something weaker?

Fixing the object of study

An earlier framing of this project named WALGA's 2018 Policy Statement on Climate Change as the document under study. That was wrong. The Policy Statement is WALGA's own sector-wide advocacy position, endorsed by State Council on 4 July 2018, and is not something individual councils sign. This project studies the separate Declaration, the instrument councils actually sign: 62 signatories, over 87 per cent of WA's population, as read from WALGA's site on 5 August 2026.

The gap, stated honestly

Two literatures sit next to this question without answering it. Bush and Doyon (2025) and Greenfield, Moloney and Granberg (2022) both study formal climate emergency declarations in Victorian councils, using frameworks built around that stronger instrument. WALGA's Declaration is explicitly not an emergency declaration. A documented search of 37 records, including one aimed specifically at WALGA's Declaration plus academic, study or thesis, found no existing study of it. That confirms the gap for this pass; it stays provisional until the same protocol is re-run against Scopus and Web of Science.

Objective 1: the literature review

Eight studies included

  • Ruiz-Campillo, Castan Broto and Westman (2021), Politics and Governance: motivations and intended outcomes across 300 declarations worldwide
  • Bush and Doyon (2025), npj Climate Action: transformative-action framework, 39 Victorian councils
  • Gudde et al. (2021), Energy Policy: delivery-plan completeness across 308 UK local authorities
  • Hicks, Davidson, Lau et al. (2025), Climatic Change: LLM-based detection of climate emergency mode, Victorian councils
  • Greenfield, Moloney and Granberg (2022), Climate: symbolic-act-versus-disruption framing, 30 Victorian councils
  • Howarth, Lane and Fankhauser (2021), Climatic Change: pathways and purposes, 28 London boroughs
  • Krause (2011), Environment and Planning C: symbolic-versus-substantive index, US municipalities in a voluntary climate-protection network
  • Schulze (2024), Policy Studies Journal: six-channel policy-diffusion framework, Hessen, Germany

Full search protocol, screening log and framework synthesis: see wa-councils-walga-climate/slr/ in the repository.

The adapted coding framework

None of the six emergency-declaration frameworks transfers to WALGA's Declaration unmodified. Several score against a bar, an asserted emergency, that the Declaration never sets. The resulting 12-item coding scheme keeps the transferable parts: named targets, responsibility, resourcing and reporting cadence, mitigation and adaptation together, cross-council collaboration, First Peoples' acknowledgement, justice and equity, and a biodiversity link, plus one item specific to this project that flags any source conflating the Declaration with the 2018 Policy Statement.

Scope and status

Objectives 1 to 3, the literature review plus two document-analysis arms (within-council before and after, and a cross-council comparison group), are the realistic four-month scope. Interviews with council officers, Objective 4, are optional, ethics-gated, and treated as a first cut to extend later.

Status right now: Objective 1 done for this pass. No council document has been gathered or coded. The project pauses here for confirmation of the included-studies list and the coding framework before Objective 2 starts.
